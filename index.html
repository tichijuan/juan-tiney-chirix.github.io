01 · Positioning

Scholarship rooted in place, movement, and relation.

I am an interdisciplinary scholar and higher-education educator specializing in Indigenous and Latin American Studies, Human Geography, and Community & Regional Planning. My research and teaching examine relationships among people, place, territory, culture, and the environment.

My academic formation across Guatemala, Cuba, and the United States shapes a practice attentive to movement, language, migration, and the uneven production of knowledge. I bring qualitative and mixed-methods approaches into conversation with Indigenous and place-based knowledge.

02 · Research

Waters, territories, and spatial memory.

My scholarship connects Indigenous epistemologies and methodologies with questions of territory, spatial memory, water, environmental change, dispossession, and community futures.

01 · DISSERTATION

Indigenous Cartographies of Water

My doctoral research, Contemporary Maya Tz’utujil Community and Santiago Atitlán’s Spatial Memory: Indigenous Cartographies of Water, examines contemporary Maya Tz’utujil relationships to Lake Atitlán through Indigenous methodologies and community-engaged research.

03 · Method

Walking the methodological shoreline.

The shoreline is both a site and a method: a space of encounter where bodies, waters, territories, memories, and knowledge become relational.

BODY

Body mapping foregrounds embodied knowledge and asks how people experience, remember, and narrate place through the body.

Methods include ethnography, walking methodology, participatory mapping, body mapping, oral histories, spatial and cultural analysis, and community-engaged research.
